Diane Foxington is the hottest animated animal in the business.

I am not a furry, and I will not tolerate any baseless claims to the contrary.

There is something almost unfair about comparing Diane Foxington to Judy Hopps, because the more you think about it, the more obvious the gap becomes. Judy is lovable, yes. She is determined, sincere, and easy to cheer for. But that is also exactly the problem: her appeal is so clean, so safe, so carefully built around being the perfect little underdog that it starts to feel almost too easy. Judy is the character you are supposed to admire. Diane Foxington is the character you cannot look away from. She walks into a scene with this effortless confidence, this polished mystery, this sense that she has lived an entire complicated life before the movie even started. And that is what makes her so much more captivating. Diane is not just charming because she is nice or hardworking; she is magnetic because she feels layered, stylish, dangerous, and human in a way Judy simply does not. Judy may be iconic, but Diane has presence. Judy inspires you. Diane pulls you in. And compared to that kind of elegance, mystery, and emotional complexity, Judy starts to feel overrated—not bad, not unlikable, but just much less fascinating than the character who quietly steals every scene she is in.
